Online Safety

Passwords, scams, and privacy — the stuff that keeps your accounts (and wallet) safe.

Password management

A strong, unique password is still your first line of defence. Here’s what actually matters.

  • –Use a different password for every important account
  • –Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it’s offered
  • –Never share your password over chat, even with friends
  • –Consider a password manager instead of memorising

Common scam patterns

Phishing emails

Urgent messages asking you to click a link and ‘verify’ your account.

Fake job or scholarship offers

Offers that ask for money upfront before you have even started.

Too-good-to-be-true deals

Prices so low they are designed to make you act before you think.

Impersonation calls

Callers pretending to be your school, bank, or even a friend in trouble.

Why privacy matters

Every account and app collects a little more about you than the last. On its own that’s harmless — combined, it’s a profile scammers and advertisers can use against you.

  • –Check privacy settings on new apps and accounts before you start using them
  • –Think twice before posting your address, timetable, or when your room will be empty
  • –Only grant an app the permissions it actually needs — deny the rest

Safe actions checklist

Habits worth building — use this as a reference while you tidy up your accounts.

Use a different password for every important account

Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it’s offered

Never share your password over chat, even with friends

Think twice before clicking a link in an unexpected message

Scam protection tips

How do I spot a phishing email? + –

Check the sender address carefully, hover over links before clicking, and be wary of urgent, threatening language.

What if I already clicked a suspicious link? + –

Don’t enter any info, close the tab, run a security scan if you’re on a computer, and change your passwords just in case.

Are scholarship offers that ask for payment legit? + –

Legitimate scholarships never require payment to receive funds. Treat any request for fees or bank details as a red flag.
